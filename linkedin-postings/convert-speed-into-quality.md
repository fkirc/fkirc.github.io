AI-Slowness does not produce quality.

That sounds wrong, but you cannot review your way to quality, because many quality-problems are not visible at review time.
Half-broken edge cases and UX-gaps often surface once someone actually uses the software. A team that is too slow to build is also too slow to fix, and the result is what I call "Death of Thousands Cut" software: software that functionally works, but is full of half-broken edge cases that nobody was ever fast enough to fix.

So instead of one fixed review policy, I propose an "AI acceleration pedal" that techleads push on a case by case basis.
A fixed "sensible midground" is not a compromise between reckless and slow, but a third failure mode: the same pedal position for every task, regardless of what the task can destroy.
What decides the pedal position is the blast radius: push the pedal harder where errors are cheap to undo, and release the pedal where they are not.

The freed up time can then be spent on the quality-issues that would otherwise be ignored and left around rotten in a "UX-backlog".
