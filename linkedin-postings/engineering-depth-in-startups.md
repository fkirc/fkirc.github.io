While planning my next career step, I was undergoing a profound mindshift about what the value of my work will be.
For multiple years, I focused on quality and on execution-speed.
It was the right thing for me. Two times in series, I transformed a startup's techstack to increase velocity. Both times, I achieved this goal through the forking of system-level packages and through low-level integration-code.
However, in the agentic era, early-stage startups seem to have entirely different needs.
I talked to a few founders, and the conversation often went like this:
"Hey Felix, I see that you are a techguy. Great. But we already AI-generated our MVP.
Now we need a person with focus on X". With "X" being marketing, sales or some specialized domain knowledge.
So then I asked myself the question:
In the agentic era, which type of company will still need a person with a deep knowledge of computer science?
I came up with the following (not complete) list:

- Large-scale enterprise backend-teams with challenges regarding concurrency, scalability, and regulations.
- Fast-growing scaleups (not early-stage startups) who need to iterate fast but do so in a secure and compliant way.
- Deep-tech startups whose product itself is the optimization of agents, harnesses, or similar.

Therefore my next career-step will likely fork off into one of those three directions.
Despite not having many years of "large-scale enterprise" in my CV, my MSc in Algorithms and IT-security might give me an entry-ticket into large-scale distributed systems engineering.
But then I made a second observation:
In the agentic era, it is unlikely that anyone will pay me X thousand euros per month only for raw quality and iteration-speed. Instead, that value is shifting towards safety and architectural mastery.
I might shift towards becoming a gatekeeper, but being a gatekeeper with a pro-active mindset.
I will always positively value the startup-community, but at the same time I also accept the reality of which type of companies a person with a deep knowledge of computer science may still bring value to the table.
