title: "Agentic Review Meltdown: Fallback to Fiduciary Trust"
---

In the [Triple Loop Condition](triple-loop-condition.md), I proposed conditions for performing engineering tasks at AI-inference-speed while preserving safety.
If all three conditions are fulfilled, human reviews can collapse to a short and efficient verification gate.
However, even if all conditions hold, how can we trust the framework guardrailing those three conditions?

### Establishing a Chain of Trust

Agents can verify other agents, establishing a chain of trust from short verification summaries to more complex verifications.
In fact, I believe agentic verifications are still heavily underused in engineering.
Also, formal methods and mathematically verifiable results might become more widespread in the agentic era.
Nevertheless, at some point the trust chain should anchor somewhere outside the agents themselves — often in a human engineer.

### Fiduciary Trust as the Last Resort to Prevent Collapse

Agents can be viewed as hyper-productive, hyper-fast employees who sound equally confident whether or not they actually understood a task — and whose output looks polished either way.
Hence, the cheap signals that reviewers traditionally relied on lose much of their diagnostic value, and distinguishing a genuinely correct solution from a superficially correct one gets expensive exactly when the volume of solutions explodes.
When systematic verification is not yet strong enough, the question of who should do a task tends to collapse to fiduciary trust: trust among peer engineers and trust among managers, and the question of who trusts whom to exercise that rigorous skepticism.
As such, switching companies might become increasingly painful because this trust needs to be rebuilt again and again, absent other checks and balances.

### New Metric: Optimizing for Review Capacity

In a world bottlenecked by review capacity, the metric to optimize is not raw task output, but rather reducing the effort required to review.
Where agentic verifications are still lagging behind, review capacity may depend on the (perceived and real) trustworthiness of the people managing those agents, who are supposed to act in their organization's best interests.

### Outlook

We should use agentic power not only to increase throughput, but to generate new quality checks wherever feasible.
The stronger our agentic verifications become over time, the more efficiently we can evaluate AI results instead of overly relying on fiduciary trust.
